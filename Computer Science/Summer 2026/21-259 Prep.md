# 21-259 Prep

Up: [[Fall 2026 Prep]]

## The actual target

This course is throughput-limited. The 6h/week of homework is mostly integral grinding and setup errors. Make the integration automatic and the region-sketching confident, and 6h becomes 3h.

Of the two courses, **this reduction is the reliable one.** 10 → 7 is very likely achievable and most of it comes from a single item below.

## Text

Stewart, *Calculus: Early Transcendentals* 8th ed. is the standard 21-259 text (some sections have used APEX Calculus Vol. 3, which is free). But your **primary spine should be Paul's Online Math Notes, Calculus III** — it matches the topic list almost exactly and every practice problem has a fully worked solution. See [[Resources]].

Chapter map: 12 (vectors) → 13 (vector functions) → 14 (partial derivatives) → 15 (multiple integrals) → 16 (vector calculus).

## What carries over from 21-122

21-122 covered Stewart ch. 7, 9, 10, 11 and parts of 8 and 12. Those pieces do **not** carry over evenly.

### Early 122 (integration techniques) — high relevance, lopsided distribution

| Technique | Frequency inside 259's multiple integrals |
|---|---|
| Polynomial / basic antiderivatives | The plurality. Must be instant, not "quick." |
| u-substitution | Constant. The workhorse. |
| Powers of sin and cos | Very high — and badly underrated |
| Integration by parts | Occasional |
| Trig substitution | Rare — and a signal you chose bad coordinates |
| Partial fractions | Nearly absent |

Powers of sine and cosine are the underrated one. Spherical coordinates hand you ∫sin φ dφ, ∫sin³φ dφ, ∫sin φ cos²φ dφ on essentially every problem; polar hands you ∫cos²θ dθ over [0, 2π] constantly. Re-deriving the half-angle identity each time costs two minutes × forty problems a week.

### Late 122 — splits hard

**Series, Taylor, Maclaurin, power series, convergence tests → essentially zero relevance.** No convergence testing, no radius of convergence. The only appearance is conceptual: the second-derivative test with the Hessian in ch. 14.7 is morally a second-order Taylor expansion. You compute nothing. Don't review it. (Not wasted study — it's load-bearing in 21-260, and the asymptotic comfort quietly helps in [[15-451 Prep|15-451]].)

**Parametric curves and polar coordinates → load-bearing infrastructure.** Not as integration *technique*, as *setup*:

- Ch. 13 vector-valued functions **are** parametric curves with a z-coordinate. **r**(t) = ⟨x(t), y(t), z(t)⟩, and arc length ∫|**r**′(t)| dt is the 122 parametric formula with a third term.
- Ch. 15.3 recasts what 122 gave you as ∫½r² dθ into ∫∫ r dr dθ. Same object, new framing — and that lone `r` in the area element is the most-forgotten symbol in the course.
- Cylindrical is polar plus z; spherical is the natural extension. If polar is reflexive, both are nearly free.
- Ch. 16 line integrals are pure parametrization start to finish.

Also: 122 already covered vectors and the dot product, so 259's ch. 12 is cheaper than it looks — only the cross product, lines and planes, and quadric surfaces are genuinely new.

## Known gap — read this first

Two separate things, and only one is a problem.

> [!check] Never covered cylindrical or spherical
> **Not a gap.** Stewart 15.7–15.8, taught around week 9 of the semester. Nobody arrives having seen them. Handled with a one-hour orientation in [[Week 2 - Data Structures and Amortized|Week 2]] and nothing more.

> [!danger] Polar and parametric are shaky
> **This is the highest-priority item in the whole prep plan**, ahead of anything in [[15-451 Prep]]. Not because it's hard — because it's a *prerequisite*. A missing unit costs you that unit; a missing prerequisite makes every later unit harder to absorb. Polar feeds ch. 15.3, cylindrical, spherical, and all of ch. 16.
>
> **The leverage:** cylindrical *is* polar — same r and θ, z riding along untouched. Fix polar now and cylindrical costs twenty minutes in October, with spherical the only genuinely new idea. Arrive with polar shaky and you're learning three things at once under deadline. That's the scenario that produces a 15-hour week in a 9-unit course.

### 259 wants a narrower slice than 122 taught

"Not good at the 122 polar chapter" ≠ "will struggle in 259." Roughly a third of it carries.

**Polar:**
1. Conversion mechanics — trivial
2. **Describing a region** — shape in, θ-bounds and r-bounds out ← the money skill, essentially the whole game
3. The area element `r dr dθ`, and never dropping the `r`
4. Recognizing *when* to go polar — circular symmetry, or x² + y² in the integrand

Conspicuously absent: graphing limaçons and roses. 122 made you plot exotic curves; 259 regions are disks, annuli, sectors, the occasional cardioid.

**Parametric:** given a geometric description ("the segment from A to B," "the circle x² + y² = 4 in the plane z = 3," "where the cylinder meets the plane"), write **r**(t) and the t-range. That's nearly all of ch. 13 and most of ch. 16.

Repair happens in [[Week 1 - DP to Reflex|Week 1]], 6h, before anything else.

## The sleeper item

> [!danger] The failure mode is setup, not arithmetic
> Nobody fails 259 saying "I can't do this integral." They fail saying **"I set up the wrong integral."** Fluency matters because it makes *iterating on the setup cheap* — try an order, watch it go ugly, back out, try another. If each attempt costs ten minutes of grinding, you'll commit to a bad setup out of sunk cost rather than abandoning it. That is where the nine-hour problem sets come from.
>
> This is [[Week 1 - DP to Reflex|Week 1]]. Do it first.
