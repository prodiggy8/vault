# 15-451 Prep

Up: [[Fall 2026 Prep]]

## The actual target

Not "know more algorithms." This:

> **Reading a problem statement produces a shortlist of 2–3 candidate techniques within 60 seconds.**

That is the entire difference between a 15h week and a 10h week. You cannot pre-solve novel problems — novelty is the point of the course. What you can do is eliminate the *re-learning* phase (the technique the problem tests, which you half-remember) and shrink the *flailing* phase by having a search procedure instead of vibes.

## Course shape (Spring 2026 instance — verify for Fall)

Four units:

1. **Dynamic programming** — knapsack, DAG, subset, prefix, interval, tree
2. **Data structures** — (lazy) segment trees, merging small into large, amortized analysis with stacks and queues, string hashing/fingerprinting
3. **Greedy, flows** — augmenting paths, capacity scaling, linear programming, duality, zero-sum games
4. **Other models of computation** — approximation, online, streaming, lower bounds, RAM model (hashing)

Grading in that instance: homework 5% (completion-graded, 6 total), oral homework 10% (4), quizzes 15% (6, min dropped), Midterm 1 20% + Midterm 2 20% + Final 30%.

> [!tip] Read that grading split again
> If Fall keeps completion-graded homework at 5%, **stop polishing writeups**. Correct and legible, not beautiful. People routinely burn 4h/week on LaTeX aesthetics for five percent of the grade. Save the polish for oral HW and exams. See [[Semester Process]].

## Note the ordering

Units 1 and 2 are both the most drillable *and* come first. Everything in this plan front-loads them deliberately — you bank hours in September and coast into the harder, less pre-learnable material with a surplus.

## Deliberately skipped

LP duality, approximation, online, streaming.

They come late, they're conceptually clean, and lecture handles them fine. Pre-learning them buys nothing — the prep hour is worth more spent on DP.
