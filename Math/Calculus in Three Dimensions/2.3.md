> [!definition] Dot product
> Given $u= \langle u_{1}, u_{2}, u_{3} \rangle$ and $v=\langle v_{1}, v_{2}, v_{3} \rangle$, the dot product is defined as:
> $$u \cdot v = u_{1}v_{1} + u_{2}v_{2} + u_{3}v_{3}$$

The dot product is commutative, distributive, associative, and $v \cdot v = |v|^2$. We can write the dot product in a different way too:
$$u \cdot v = |u||v| \cos \theta$$
Where $\theta$ is the angle between the two vectors. Hence, we can find such angle by solving the dot product:
$$\theta = \arccos\left( \frac{u \cdot v}{|u||v|} \right)$$
Two vectors are **orthogonal** if and only if $u \cdot v = 0$.

We say orthogonal for vectors, perpendicular for lines, and normal usually when describing the angle made with a plane or surface.

> [!definition] Projection
> A vector projection of $v$ onto $u$ is the vector labeled $\operatorname{proj}_{u}v$. It has the same initial point as both $v$ and $u$ and same direction as $u$. It represents the component of $v$ that acts in the direction of $u$.
> $$\operatorname{proj}_{u}v = \frac{u \cdot v}{|u|^2}u$$

Decomposing a vector $v$ into two orthogonal components:
1. We first take $p = \operatorname{proj}_{u}v$ for any vector $u$
2. Then we define $q = v - p$ which is orthogonal to $p$.
