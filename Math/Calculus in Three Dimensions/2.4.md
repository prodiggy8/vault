> [!definition] Cross product
> The *cross product* finds a vector orthogonal to two given vectors. Let $u=\langle u_{1}, u_{2}, u_{3} \rangle$ and $v=\langle v_{1}, v_{2}, v_{3} \rangle$, the cross product is defined as:
> $$u \times v = \langle u_{2}v_{3} - u_{3}v_{2}, u_{3}v_{1} - u_{1}v_{3}, u_{1}v_{2} - u_{2}v_{1} \rangle$$

The cross product is **anticommutative**: $u \times v = -(v \times u)$.
Scalar multiplication also changes: $c(u \times v) = cu \times v = u \times cv$.

The cross product of standard unit vectors can be useful sometimes:
$$i \times i = j \times j = k \times k = 0$$
$$
\begin{align}
i \times j = k && j \times i = -k \\
j \times k = i && k \times j = -i \\
k \times i = j && i \times k = -j
\end{align}
$$
**Magnitude:** $|u \times v| = |u||v|\sin \theta$ where $\theta$ is the angle between $u$ and $v$.

#### Determinants

The formula for cross product is quite difficult to memorize. We can use the determinant notation. Reminder of how to do determinants:
$$\begin{vmatrix}a_{1} & a_{2} \\ b_{1} & b_{2}\end{vmatrix}=a_{1}b_{2}-b_{1}a_{2}$$
$$
\begin{vmatrix}
a_{1} & a_{2} & a_{3} \\
b_{1} & b_{2} & b_{3} \\
c_{1} & c_{2}  & c_{3}
\end{vmatrix} = 
a_{1} \begin{vmatrix}
b_{2} & b_{3} \\ c_{2} & c_{3}
\end{vmatrix}
- a_{2} \begin{vmatrix}
b_{1} & b_{3} \\ c_{1} & c_{3}
\end{vmatrix}
+ a_{3} \begin{vmatrix}
b_{1} & b_{2} \\ c_{1} & c_{2}
\end{vmatrix}
$$
Now for the cross product:
$$ u \times v =
\begin{vmatrix}
i & j & k \\
u_{1} & u_{2} & u_{3} \\
v_{1} & v_{2} & v_{3}
\end{vmatrix} =
i \begin{vmatrix}
u_{2} & u_{3} \\
v_{2} & v_{3}
\end{vmatrix}
- j \begin{vmatrix}
u_{1} & u_{3} \\
v_{1} & v_{3}
\end{vmatrix}
+ k \begin{vmatrix}
u_{1} & u_{2} \\
v_{1} & v_{3}
\end{vmatrix}
$$
#### Applications

##### Area of Parallelogram

If we locate $u$ and $v$ such that they form sides of a parallelogram, we can find the area using $|u \times v|=|u||v|\sin \theta$ given $\theta$, the angle between them.

##### Area of Triangle

Half of the area of the parallelogram.

#### Triple Scalar Product
$$
w \cdot (u \times v) = \begin{vmatrix}
w_{1} & w_{2} & w_{3} \\
u_{1} & u_{2} & u_{3} \\
v_{1} & v_{2} & v_{3}
\end{vmatrix}
$$
##### Volume of Parallelepiped

Given adjacent edges $u$, $v$, and $w$, we have $V=|u \cdot (v \times w)|$ where $V$ is absolute valued (not a magnitude).
