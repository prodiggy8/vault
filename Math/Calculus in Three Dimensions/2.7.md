## Spherical Coordinates

The same way the Cartesian coordinates can be extended to three dimensions, the polar coordinate system also can, with $(r, \theta, z)$, where:
- $(r, \theta)$ are polar coordinates
- $z$ is the usual $z$-coordinate from the Cartesian system
$$
\begin{align}
x=r\cos \theta \\
y=r\sin \theta \\
z = z \\
r^2 = x^2 + y^2 \\ \\
\tan \theta = \frac{y}{x} \\
r = \sqrt{ x^2 + y^2 } \\
\theta = \arctan\left( \frac{y}{x} \right)
\end{align}
$$
When $z$ is held constant in this system we get a plane parallel to $xy$-plane. When $r$ is held constant we get a cylinder.
When $\theta$ is held constant we get a half-plane starting from the $z$-axis.
