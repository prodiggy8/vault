## Spherical Coordinates

The same way the Cartesian coordinates can be extended to three dimensions, the polar coordinate system also can, with $(r, \theta, z)$, where:
- $(r, \theta)$ are polar coordinates
- $z$ is the usual $z$-coordinate from the Cartesian system
$$
\begin{align}
x=r\cos \theta \\
y=r\sin \theta \\
z = z \\
r^2 = x^2 + y^2 \\ \\
r = \sqrt{ x^2 + y^2 } \\
\theta = \arctan\left( \frac{y}{x} \right) \\
z = z
\end{align}
$$
When $z$ is held constant in this system we get a plane parallel to $xy$-plane. When $r$ is held constant we get a cylinder.
When $\theta$ is held constant we get a half-plane starting from the $z$-axis.

## Spherical Coordinates

A point $P$ is represented by the triple $(\rho, \theta, \phi)$ where:
- $\rho$ is the (3D) distance between $P$ and the origin
- $\theta$ is the angle from the positive $x$-axis
- $\phi$ is the angle formed by the positive $z$-axis and line segment $OP$, where $O$ is the origin and $0 \leq \phi \leq \pi$.

**Converting to Cartesian**
$$
\begin{align}
x = \rho \sin \phi \cos \theta \\
y = \rho \sin \phi \sin \theta \\
z = \rho \cos \phi \\ \\
p^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 \\
\theta = \arctan(\frac{y}{x}) \\
\phi = \arccos\left( \frac{z}{\sqrt{ \rho^2 }} \right)
\end{align}
$$
**Converting to cylindrical**
$$
\begin{align}
r = \rho \sin \phi \\
\theta = \theta \\
z = \rho \cos \phi \\ \\
\rho = \sqrt{ r^2 + z^2 } \\
\theta = \theta \\
\phi = \arccos\left( \frac{z}{\rho} \right)
\end{align}
$$
