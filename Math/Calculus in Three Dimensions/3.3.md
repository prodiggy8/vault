# Arc Length

It’s just the magnitude of the derivative!

$$
s=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}} \sqrt{ (f'(t))^2 + (g'(t))^2 + (h'(t))^2 } dt = \int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}} |r'(t)| dt
$$

It is also sometimes used for defining **displacement** of a particle by parametrize $s(t)$.

We can also solve $s(t)$ and find an expression using $t$. Then we can substitute $t$ for an expression parameterized on $s$.

Example:

$$
\begin{align}
r(t)&=4 \cos t i+4\sin tj \\
s(t)&= \int_{a}^t |r'(u)|du \\
s(t)&=\int_{a}^t \sqrt{(-4\sin u)^2 + (4\cos u)^2 }du \\
s(t)&=4t \\
r(s)&=4\cos \frac{si}{4} + 4\sin \frac{sj}{4}
\end{align}
$$

### Curvature

Curvature is a way to measure how sharply a curve turns. A circle has constant curvature. The smaller the radius of the circle, the greater the  curvature.

Let $C$ be a smooth curve given by $r(s)$, where $s$ is the arc-length parameter. The **curvature** $\kappa$ at $s$ is:

$$\kappa=|\frac{dT}{ds}|$$
Remembering that $T=\frac{r'(t)}{|r'(t)|}$ is the tangent function for that curve.
Other ways of calculating curvature:
$$
\begin{align}
\kappa &= \frac{|T'(t)|}{|r'(t)|} \\ \\
\kappa &= \frac{|r'(t) \times r''(t)|}{|r'(t)|^3} \\ \\
\kappa &= \frac{|y''|}{\left[ 1 + (y')^2 \right]^\frac{3}{2}}
\end{align}
$$

### Normal and Binormal Vectors

Let $C$ be a 3D **smooth** curve, then the principal unit normal vector at $t$ is defined to be:
$$N(t)=\frac{T'(t)}{|T'(t)|}$$
The binormal vector is defined as:
$$B(t)=T(t) \times N(t)$$

Meaning of all of these:
- $T(t)$ is where the curve is going
- $N(t)$ is which way the curve is turning
- $\kappa$ is how **fast** the turning is happening
- $B(t)$ is the axis the bending happens around
	- $B$ is orthogonal to both $T$ and $N$.

The **normal plane** is perpendicular to the curve at any moment and is formed by $N$ and $B$ as axes.

These three together are called the **Frenet frame of reference**.
