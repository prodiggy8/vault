# Arc Length

It’s just the magnitude of the derivative!
$$
s=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}} \sqrt{ (f'(t))^2 + (g'(t))^2 + (h'(t))^2 } dt = \int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}} |r'(t)| dt
$$
It is also sometimes used for defining **displacement** of a particle by parametrize $s(t)$.

We can also solve $s(t)$ and find an expression using $t$. Then we can substitute $t$ for an expression parameterized on $s$.

Example:
$$
\begin{align}
r(t)&=4 \cos t i+4\sin tj \\
s(t)&= \int_{a}^t |r'(u)|du \\
s(t)&=\int_{a}^t \sqrt{(-4\sin u)^2 + (4\cos u)^2 }du \\
s(t)&=4t \\
r(s)&=4\cos \frac{si}{4} + 4\sin \frac{sj}{4}
\end{align}
$$
### Curvature

Let $C$ be a smooth curve given by $r(s)$, where $s$ is the arc-length parameter. The **curvature** $\kappa$ at $s$ is:
$$\kappa=|\frac{dT}{ds}|=|\frac{dT}{ds} \frac{ds}{dt}|$$
Remembering that $T=\frac{r'(t)}{|r'(t)|}$ is the tangent function for that curve. Also:
$$
\begin{align}
\kappa &= \frac{|T'(t)|}{|r'(t)|} && \text{as follows from Chain Rule} \\
\kappa &= \frac{|r'(t) \times r''(t)|}{|r'(t)|^3}
\end{align}
$$
